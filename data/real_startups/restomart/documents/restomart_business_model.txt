RESTOMART — BUSINESS MODEL AND OPERATIONS

Business
--------
RestoMart is a B2B food-supply and restaurant procurement company.
The company serves restaurants rather than individual consumers.
The core customer problem is fragmented and operationally inefficient restaurant procurement.

Customer
--------
Primary customers: Restaurants
Reported customer base: More than 165 restaurants

Procurement Model
-----------------
RestoMart uses a curated SKU approach.
The model is intended to improve procurement efficiency, product availability,
inventory management, delivery reliability, and supply-chain economics.

Cluster Strategy
----------------
The operating model uses restaurant density within geographic clusters.
Higher customer density can improve delivery, fulfillment, inventory positioning,
customer service, and route-planning economics.

Micro-Fulfillment
-----------------
RestoMart uses micro-fulfillment centers as part of its operating model.
The centers support localized inventory and rapid fulfillment.
The target delivery proposition is approximately four hours.

Fulfillment
-----------
Reported fulfillment reliability: Approximately 95%.
The business model therefore places significant emphasis on operational execution
and supply-chain reliability.

Wastage
-------
The business has been described as having very low wastage.
This is relevant because inventory loss and spoilage can materially affect gross margin.

Unit Economics
--------------
The most important unit-economic variables include:
- Restaurant acquisition cost
- Average order value
- Orders per restaurant
- Gross margin per order
- Delivery cost per order
- Fulfillment cost
- Inventory carrying cost
- Wastage
- Restaurant retention
- Contribution margin

Key Scalability Question
------------------------
The central operating question is whether the cluster-density and micro-fulfillment
model becomes increasingly efficient as restaurant density increases.

The investment analysis should distinguish:
1. Revenue growth
2. Gross-margin improvement
3. Contribution-margin improvement
4. Customer retention
5. Fulfillment efficiency

Competitive Considerations
--------------------------
Potential competitive pressure can come from:
- Traditional distributors
- Wholesale markets
- Restaurant procurement aggregators
- Digital B2B marketplaces
- Direct supplier relationships
- Large food-supply platforms

The available source information does not establish a complete competitive landscape
or quantified competitor comparison.
